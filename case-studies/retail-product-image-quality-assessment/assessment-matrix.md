# Retail Image Assessment Matrix

This matrix summarizes the criteria and findings used in the Retail Product Image Quality and Visual Inspection case study.

| Assessment dimension | What was reviewed | Observation | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product visibility | Distinguishability of products from neighboring products and surrounding objects | Products, shelf rows and major product shapes were distinguishable | Suitable for basic visual identification |
| Shelf alignment | Visibility and consistency of shelf rows and product arrangement | Shelf rows remained visible and products generally followed the shelf structure | Overall shelf organization was understandable |
| Packaging boundaries | Visibility of package outlines and shapes | Packaging outlines remained visible and package boundaries could generally be distinguished | Packaging structure appeared sufficiently preserved for basic visual identification |
| Repeated visual patterns | Similar or repetitive details that may need closer inspection | Some product details appeared visually similar on closer inspection | Further inspection warranted; duplication was not confirmed |
| Text quality | Readability and visual integrity of packaging text | Small text required closer examination and could not be reliably verified from the available view | Text quality not fully determined |
| Image sharpness | Clarity of product outlines and visible detail | Product outlines remained visible and the image appeared sufficiently clear for general review | Adequate for general visual inspection |
| Object separation | Distinction between adjacent products | Adjacent products could generally be differentiated; no obvious large-scale merging was observed | Object separation generally maintained |

## Interpretation

The assessment supports a qualitative conclusion about the image's usability for basic visual review. It does not establish that every product is correctly identified or that the image satisfies a commercial, planogram, OCR or model-performance threshold.

## Reporting discipline

- An observation records what was visible in the image.
- An assessment explains what that observation supports.
- A potential issue identifies something that warrants more inspection.
- A confirmed defect requires sufficient evidence to distinguish it from normal visual similarity or image content.
